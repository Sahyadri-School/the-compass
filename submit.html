Contribute

Write for The Compass

The Compass is a collaborative platform. If you're a teacher or student with a mathematical or scientific idea worth sharing, we'd like to hear from you.

Who can submit

We welcome submissions from mathematics and science teachers at the high school or middle school level, and from students who want to share a finding, a proof, an experiment, or an observation. You don't need to be affiliated with a KFI school to contribute — the magazine is open to anyone with something worth sharing with fellow teachers and learners.

What we're looking for

Anything that would interest a curious teacher or student. Some examples of what has appeared, and what we'd welcome more of:

geometry number theory algebra puzzles pedagogy science experiments history of math classroom activities
  • Original problems, proofs, or elegant solutions to known problems
  • Classroom activities or teaching approaches that worked well
  • Short expository pieces explaining a mathematical or scientific idea
  • Puzzles, games, or recreational mathematics
  • Reflections on how a topic connects across subjects, or to everyday life
  • Science "snippets" — short, accessible summaries of interesting findings, with a source

Guidelines

  • Send your piece as a PDF or Word document. Diagrams and images should be included at good resolution, either embedded or attached separately.
  • There's no strict length requirement — a short puzzle and a multi-page article are both welcome — but most pieces run 500–2,500 words.
  • Cite sources for any facts, data, or quotations you didn't originate yourself.
  • Include your name, school or affiliation (if any), and a line or two about yourself for the contributor credit.

How to submit

  1. Email your piece to compass.math.2024@gmail.com with the subject line "Compass submission — [your title]".
  2. The editorial team reads every submission and will get back to you — typically within a few weeks.
  3. If accepted, we may suggest edits before publication, in a future issue of the magazine.

Published pieces are shared under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license — free for other teachers and students to use, share, and build on with credit, for non-commercial purposes. You keep your copyright; this just sets the terms under which readers can use your published piece.

Not sure if your idea fits? Send a short note describing it before writing the whole piece — we're happy to give early feedback so you don't spend time on something that isn't the right fit.
